Privacy

Last updated 20 November 2026

stream-thing sync keeps the DizziTV app (stream-thing) in step across your own phones, TVs and PCs. This page says, in plain words, what it keeps and why.

What is stored with your account

  • From “Sign in with Google” (in the app or on this website): your Google account ID, email address, name and profile picture link. Nothing else from your Google account is requested.
  • The API keys you choose to save (TorBox, Real-Debrid, MDBList, aioratings poster key) and which debrid service you use. Keys are encrypted before they are written to disk.
  • Your watch progress, My list, profiles, settings and installed addons.
  • The names of the devices you sign in to the app on, and when each was signed in and last used.

So you can sign in on another of your devices and pick up where you were.

What is popular

The watch progress and My list that accounts sync are also added up across all accounts, to see what is watched: which titles, kinds, genres and years, at what times of day, and how far through people get. Only totals are looked at (“this show was watched by 12 accounts”), never one account's history, and they are seen only by the people who run DizziTV. To name a title, its ID alone is looked up in a public catalogue. If you would rather not be part of these totals, use the app without an account: nothing you watch is then sent anywhere.

Anonymous usage counts

Separately from any account, the app sends anonymous counts about once a day, so we can see what it is used for:

  • which sections were opened, and how many times;
  • how many things were played, how many failed to play, and for how many minutes, by kind (film, episode, live channel, sport, esports);
  • which features were used (watch party, downloads, reminders, the bug button);
  • the kind of device (for example “Android TV”), the app's version, and roughly how it is set up: signed in or not, which debrid service, about how many addons, and roughly how long the app has been installed.

The report carries no account, no device ID and nothing that links one report to the next. It never includes titles, searches, addon addresses, or anything about channels you made yourself. It is sent without your sign-in. The server adds the numbers to daily totals; it keeps no record of single reports and does not store the IP address they came from (the address is used, in memory, only to stop one sender flooding the counts).

Live channel viewers

While you watch one of the app's own live channels, the app tells the server which channel once a minute, with a random pass it makes up when you tune in and throws away when you leave. The server holds these in memory for a couple of minutes to count how many are watching, and writes down only each channel's daily totals. Channels you made yourself are never named.

Turning it off

In the app: Settings → About → “Share anonymous usage counts”. With it off, the app sends no counts and is not counted as watching a channel.

Bug reports

Only when you press the bug button and send one: your message, a screenshot of the app, and the app's recent logs. If you are signed in, your email is attached so the report can be followed up.

Sharing

Your data is not shared with anyone and is never sold.

Cookies and tracking

There are no ads, and nothing follows you as a person: the only analytics are the anonymous counts above. The website sets one cookie: a session cookie that keeps you signed in. Google's sign-in button is loaded from Google, which handles the sign-in itself under Google's own privacy policy.

Deleting your data

Sign in on this site and choose “Delete account”. Your account, saved keys, watch progress, devices and sessions are deleted immediately. You can also sign out individual devices at any time. The anonymous counts are totals with nothing of yours in them, so there is nothing of yours to delete there. Deleting your account takes your watch history out of the totals of what is popular.
